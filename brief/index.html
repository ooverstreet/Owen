How this becomes a business

Bitcoin explorers were free and crowded. Mining pools made money because hashpower flowed through them (about 1–2%). The Bittensor analog is stake flowing through a validator. This UI is how people trust that flow. No take-rate until there is a named validator and a wallet-signed extra — not before.

How this actually works

The chain does not run the AI. It keeps score, moves money, and ejects the shops nobody wants to fund.

Trust, before a single τ moves

  • We never ask for a seed phrase, private key, or wallet JSON. If a screen ever does, it is not this app.
  • Lookup is a GET to a public indexer. Your address is already on the chain; we do not “collect” it as an account.
  • No stake, unstake, or transfer in this build. The first signing extra will name the validator and the take-rate in the confirmation itself.
  • Briefs are editorial. Live prices are chain data. We label claimed revenue so it cannot hide as fact.

The three jobs

  • Miners do the work: inference, GPUs, forecasts, agents, science.
  • Validators test that work and submit scores. Those scores become rewards.
  • Owners write the rules of the shop. They take about 18% of subnet emissions; miners and validators split the rest.

Why staking feels like venture, not yield

When you stake TAO into a subnet you swap into that subnet’s alpha token. You can earn more alpha and still lose TAO if the shop’s price falls. Root staking is the conservative park. Direct subnet staking is an active bet on one team’s market.

The six questions that matter

  1. Is there a live product a stranger can use today?
  2. Do customers pay in dollars, or only in emissions?
  3. Can anyone outside the team verify usage (OpenRouter, Polymarket, a public dashboard)?
  4. Is the team public, shipping, and still around?
  5. Is liquidity thick enough that you can exit without wrecking the pool?
  6. Is the owner selling alpha faster than the product is earning?

Most of the 128 shops fail several of those. A handful pass. That gap is the whole market.

Do not build another explorer

taostats, TaoMarketCap, SubnetAIQ, SubnetRadar, SubnetStats, and TAOFlute already cover prices, explorers, health scores, and whale tape. A prettier table is not a business — it is a Bitcoin block explorer in 2012: useful, crowded, and hard to charge for.

Bittensor is early in the same way Bitcoin was early: TAO is the bet on the network, and most of the money in the next phase goes to picks-and-shovels that sit on flow (stake, inference, attention), not on screenshots of the chain.

Where we actually get paid

  1. Validator desk (primary, Bitcoin-pool analog). Most TAO is still in Root because allocating across 128 alpha tokens feels like becoming a VC. The needed service is a conservative sleeve plus a research sleeve, executed through a validator we run or partner with. Revenue is the protocol commission on dividends — already native, already how the chain pays judges. The UI is customer acquisition, not the SKU. See the Desk tab.
  2. Front door to using a subnet (merchant analog). Charge people who do not care about TAO: inference, authenticity checks, coding agents. Chutes, Ridgeline, and Dippy are the pattern. Analytics is the wedge; usage is the company.
  3. Do not sell the map. Paid pro dashboards exist and will keep existing. Competing with free taostats on columns is how you work for free.

Not financial advice. Emissions, alpha prices, and teams change fast. Briefs are dated August 2026 and should be treated as a starting point, not a score. Live numbers come from the public Metagraphed registry (metagraph.sh), which reads the Bittensor chain.
